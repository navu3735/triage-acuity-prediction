# ESI Level 1 — Resuscitation
Patients requiring immediate life-saving intervention. Examples: cardiac arrest, severe respiratory distress, profound shock. Workflow: activate resuscitation bay, continuous monitoring, airway/IV access immediately, notify attending physician and rapid response.

# ESI Level 2 — Emergent
High-risk situations, severe pain/distress, or confusion. Examples: chest pain concerning for ACS, stroke symptoms, severe asthma. Workflow: place in monitored bed within ~10 minutes, ECG within 10 minutes for chest pain, early labs and imaging, escalate if unstable.

# ESI Level 3 — Urgent
Stable patients needing multiple resources (labs, imaging, IV meds). Examples: abdominal pain, moderate dehydration, febrile illness with comorbidity. Workflow: triage to treatment area, order indicated workup, reassess every 30–60 minutes while waiting.

# ESI Level 4 — Less Urgent
One resource expected. Examples: simple laceration, isolated sprain, UTI symptoms without systemic signs. Workflow: may wait longer; prepare for focused exam and single diagnostic or procedure.

# ESI Level 5 — Non-Urgent
No ED resources expected beyond exam/prescription. Examples: medication refill, minor rash, chronic stable complaint. Workflow: consider fast-track or redirect to urgent care / primary care when appropriate.

# Chest Pain Pathway
For suspected ACS: obtain 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival, continuous cardiac monitoring, IV access, aspirin if not contraindicated, troponin and basic labs, notify cardiology for STEMI criteria. Keep NPO until disposition clear.

# Shortness of Breath Pathway
Assess airway and SpO2 immediately. Provide supplemental O2 to target SpO2 94–98% (88–92% if known COPD). Consider nebulizers for wheeze, ABG/VBG if severe, chest X-ray, and early escalation for silent chest or altered mentation.

# Fever / Infection Workflow
Document immunocompromise and recent travel. Obtain vitals trends, consider sepsis screen (qSOFA/SIRS). For suspected sepsis: cultures before antibiotics when possible, lactate, fluids, broad-spectrum antibiotics per protocol within 1 hour for septic shock.

# Operational Throughput Guidance
Reduce door-to-doc time by routing ESI 4–5 to fast-track. Cohort boarding patients when ICU beds are limited. Monitor LWBS rate; if rising above 3%, open surge staffing and expand triage capacity. Prioritize lab STAT turnaround under 30 minutes for ESI 1–2.

# Disposition Recommendations
Admit when unstable vitals persist, high-risk chest pain, hypoxia requiring ongoing O2, or social barriers to safe discharge. Discharge with clear return precautions when vitals stable, workup negative for emergencies, and follow-up arranged.
